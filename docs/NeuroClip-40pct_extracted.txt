ABSTRACT 
The efficient retrieval of certain information on the long -form video material is a vital 
challenge within the rapidly developing framework of digital media. The workflows 
existing in use are not optimized in computing power and often an identical search has to 
be reprocessed each time the search occurs. NeuroClip can solve these fundamental 
deficiencies through the provision of an intelligent, intent aware video processing platform 
which has the capability of deriving knowledge that are searchable as transcripts. The 
project is a major designation of operational efficiency in the fields of educa tion through 
the conversion of traditional video archives into dynamic and queryable display of video 
information resource and while also focusing on semantic context rather than on mere 
keyword hits. The approach being employed is transcript-based methodology where audio 
is transcribed, then the information is turned into a form of a vect or that is useful in 
accessing the context and then this information is subjected to a windowed search 
mechanism with optional cross -encoder re -ranking to construct coherent clips. The 
technical implementation is based on a stack that is modern: a responsive web interface that 
is written using React and a scalable ba ckend using FastAPI. Core intelligence makes use 
of sentence-transformers (e.g. MiniLM-L6-v2) and pgvector implemented in SupaBase to 
efficiently store vectors, and ffmpeg+ to daily conduct accu rate clip extraction and 
streaming. System test entailed both black-box parameter sweeps and grounded relevance 
tests to test the retrieval accuracy under different window sizes as well as window strid e 
configurations. Latency Coherence Evaluations were do ne on a standard workstation (4+ 
core processor, 16GB RAM). The experimental findings have proven that with NeuroClip, 
history queries can be answered in under a second and clips related to contextual level can 
be retrieved in several seconds. Although eff ective, the existing system has its 
shortcomings, especially its relying on the quality of Automated Speech Recognition 
(ASR) the transcript of which can lead to the deterioration of search accuracy. To improve 
on these problems, future directions include supporting visual cues with multi -modal 
embeddings, supporting query rewrites with Large Language Models (LLMs) to streamline 
and support real-time generation of clips of video.

  Introduction 
   Video is the media that is the most successful in the fast changing digital media landscape, 
as the means through which information spreads, people are entertained and even watched 
over. The explosion of video material on learning platforms, company repositories and 
social networks has brou ght about a tremendous necessity of effective retrieval systems 
which extend beyond basic system of metadata filtering. Although this may cause a rise, it 
still does not mean that it is easy to access certain information within long -form video 
content. Conventional video search systems largely depend on keyword matching which is 
by its nature fragile, and fails to meet the semantic context of a query by the user. This 
usually ends up retrieving piece meal or irrelevant fragments which do not effectively 
answer the purpose of the user. Moreover, the traditional work process entails manual 
sifting through volumes of footage and this process is tedious and can be subject to errors. 
The current solutions are also ineffective in the use of computer time as it is frequently 
necessary to reform videos and reread them every time new search is needed hence wasting 
precious computer time. It is these limitations that indicate that there is now an immediate 
requirement to have an intelligent, intent -aware system capable  of extracting searchable 
knowledge within video content, but that puts emphasis in context and topic coverage, as 
opposed to keyword hits. 
NeuroClip fills these very important gaps by adding a powerful, end -to-end video 
processing solution which can conve rt stagnant video collections into interactively 
queryable assets. The system incorporates high -quality pipeline of video ingestion, 
automated transcription and sentence level embedding computation to enable semantic 
deep comprehension. The windowed retrie val process with optional cross -encoder 
reranking, in contrast to existing keyword -based tools, which tend to deliver very 
fragmented retrievals, is that NeuroClip uses a windowed retrieval process into coherent, 
context-dependent clips. This system has the benefit that it uses a common job identifier to 
unify video metadata, embeddings and history, which enables computationally expensive 
artifacts to be stored and reused. This is markedly better than conventional workflows as it 
relies on no redundancy in processing which increases the reproducibility and efficiency of 
work among the users in academic, business and surveillance fields. 
  Project Domain 
    NeuroClip works at the edge of Artificial Intelligence (AI), Deep Learning and Computer 
Vision in parti cular in the smart summaries and retrieval of video content. The project

scrubbing and search limitations with keywords. This solution helps to bridge the gap

  Problem Identification 
   Current video retrieval systems primarily utilize metadata tags or basic keyword 
matching to locate content within large archives, with some platforms offering limited 
transcript-based search cap abilities. However, these traditional approaches are inherently 
brittle; they fail to grasp the user's actual intent or the semantic context behind a query, 
often resulting in the retrieval of disjointed, short, or irrelevant video fragments that do not 
fully address the user's needs. Furthermore, users are typically forced to manually scrub 
through hours of footage to locate specific information, which is a labor -intensive and 
error-prone process that significantly hampers productivity. From a computationa l 
standpoint, existing workflows often suffer from severe inefficiency, requiring videos to be 
reprocessed or re -analyzed for every new search instance, leading to redundant resource 
usage. This project aims to address these limitations by developing a context-aware system 
that understands semantic meaning, assembles coherent video clips rather than fragmented 
hits, and optimizes computational resources through the reuse of stored embeddings. 
 Proposed Solution 
   The proposed solution, NeuroClip, is an enco mpassing video processing platform 
aimed to turn static video archives into dynamic and searchable knowledge bases through 
the use of adva nced techniques of vector embedding . The whole process of video 
ingestion, transcription, and vectorization is automated, and is transformed into sentence-
level embeddings that capture the meaning of the video, not just the keywords. Unlike a 
traditional search tool, NeuroClip leverages a complex windowed retrieval mechanism 
with cross-encoder reranking for identifying, r anking, and merging the relevant video 
segments into coherent and playable video clips that reflect the intent of the user 
accurately. To solve computational inefficiencies, the solution applies a strong storage 
4  
architecture identified by unique job identi fiers to store and reuse the expensive 
embeddings for future queries. This approach not only dramatically reduces processing 
time but also a scalable and responsive environment for the user to explore and retrieve 
specific information from a long form content. 
 Objectives 
The key objectives of NeuroClip are: 
 To design a system that delivers intent-aware clip retrieval by prioritizing semantic 
context and topic coverage over traditional brittle keyword matching.  
 To develop a comprehensive video processing pl atform that integrates automated 
transcription, sentence-level embedding, and configurable parameters for precise clip 
generation.  
 To improve the efficiency of video analysis workflows by enabling the caching and 
reuse of stored embeddings, thereby eliminating the need for redundant reprocessing.  
 To reduce the time and effort required to locate specific information within long -
form video content through a windowed semantic search mechanism.  
 To increase the reproducibility of search results by implementin g a robust history 
management system keyed by stable job identifiers.  
To validate the developed system through rigorous parameter sweep testing and 
grounded relevance assessments to ensure high retrieval accuracy and sub -second 
latency. 
 Scope of the Project  
   The scope of NeuroClip includes the creation of a full -stack web application that will 
help to facilitate the intelligent retrieval and management of video contents. The 
boundary of this system is marked by the integration of its core pipeline combining video 
ingestion, automated speech to text transcription and generation of sentence -level 
embeddings for deep semantic search capability. Key functionalities provided by the 
system are the ability to upload different formats of videos, the ability to perform context-
aware queries using natural language, and the ability to automatically generate and stream 
coherent video clips based on user intent. The system is intended to function in the web-
based environment, making use of a reactive React frontend fo r interacting with the 
system and a FastAPI backend for processing, keeping accessibility available in common

the training of a proprietary ASR model, or the u se of some complex non -linear video 
editing workflow. 
  Effectiveness of the System 
   Unlike traditional video search functionalities that rely on exact match with keywords, 
which may lead to fragmented and irrelevant results, NeuroClip brings a new paradigm by 
using the concept of deep semantic understanding for understanding the intention of the 
user. This contribution in effect closes the gap between static storage of content and 
dynamic retrieval of information, which has a clear advantage over manual s crubbing 
methods that are time consuming and error-prone. The main usefulness of the system is in 
the ability to dramatically reduce the time and cognitive effort needed to extract specific 
information from long form video archives. NeuroClip is a priceless resource to any sort of 
application, whether it be in educational research where students need to extract and 
assemble specific lecture content, corporate integration where efficient meeting 
transcription is required, or the media sector where fast conte nt extraction is necessary. 
Furthermore, the architecture of the system guarantees operational efficiency by reusing 
the embeddings that are stored, making it a very efficient and scalable system for managing 
large digital video libraries.

The current probl em is the use of different and separate tools for the various video

 Related Literature Review 
   Reimers and Gurevych (2019) proposed an adaptation of pretrained BERT network into 
an siamese and triplet network called Sentence -BERT (SBERT), which is used to obtain 
semantically meaningful sentence embeddings. The article tries to deal with the enormous 
computational cost of doing clustering and semantic search with raw BERT, with the fastest 
time to find the most similar pair in a set of 10,000 sentences being almost 65 hours. Their 
results proved that SBERT was capable of decreasing this inference time to about 5 
seconds, and still high accuracy on semantic textua l similarity (STS) benchmarks. A 
drawback that can be seen in this work, however, is that although SBERT is very efficient 
in first-stage retrieval (Bi-Encoder), it sometimes does not have the fine -grained retrieval 
characteristic of full Cross -Encoder arc hitectures when dealing with complex, nuanced 
queries in which the interaction between the query and document has to be deep. 
Nogueira and Cho (2019) studied the use of BERT in Passage Re-ranking with BERT and 
effectively used the Cross-Encoder architecture to enhance the precision of retrieval. They 
suggested in their work that the query and the candidate passage be fed together into the 
Transformer model to predict a relevance score, and through which the self -attention 
mechanism can model the interaction between query terms and document contents entirely. 
The results showed that this technique has the highest possible state of art even on such 
datasets as MS MARCO and it has much better results on Mean Average Precision (MAP) 
as compared to traditional BM 25 and vector -only methods. The main weakness that has 
been pointed out in this paper is the large latency of computations; since the query and 
document are to be run simultaneously, the system cannot afford to pre -compress the 
representational of the documents, and thus, the system is prohibitively slow to run on large 
collections, without a filtering step beforehand.

 Identified Problem from Existing Work 
   A critical analysis of the reviewed literature and existing video search applications shows 
that there are significant limitations in both retrieval accuracy and computation efficiency. 
Existing commercial platforms are largely based on brittle keyword matching techniques 
that do not account for the semantic nuance of user queries, and this leads to irre levant or 
broken up segments of video with necessary context. Technically, as we have seen in the 
case of architectures such as BERT and Cross -Encoders, there is always a clear trade -off 
between high-precision models, which have prohibitive latency, and fa ster models of the 
Bi-Encoder type, which sometimes do not capture subtle interactions between the queries 
and documents. Furthermore, from a non -functional point of view, the current workflows 
are highly inefficient; the current systems lack proper cachin g mechanisms for the 
embeddings, requiring the systems to re -process long form videos redundantly for each 
new search, which results in unacceptable delays and resource wastage. NeuroClip fills 
these research gaps by implementing a hybrid search pipeline combining the speed of pre-
computed embeddings and the precision of reranking, and introducing a stable job -based 
caching architecture. This way, videos will be processed once and queried immediately, 
which is an effective balance between high semantic accuracy and system responsiveness. 
 Selected Boundary for Proposed Solution 
   To overcome the lack of semantic context and computational inefficiency identified in 
Section 2.3, NeuroClip will use a semantic search engine that can understand the natural 
language queries via sentence level embeddings. The system will include an automated 
pipeline for video transcription, video vectorization and windowed retrieval to guarantee 
context-aware results as well as persistent storage mechanism to cache these artifacts using 
unique job ID to avoid redundant processing and support instant re trieval for subsequent 
queries. 
On the other hand, the proposed system will not be able to support streaming video analysis 
in real-time, due to the latency that currently exists in hi gh-precision reranking models, 
specifically focusing on the batch processing of the uploaded files. Additionally, the project 
scope clearly excludes the ability to train proprietary Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) 
models and instead uses proven pre -trained libraries and will not provide advanced non -
linear video editing capabilities like transitions or visual effects but will be limited to the 
ability to accurately extract a clip and simple playback.

 System Modules 
   System modules represent different functional units of the software architecture, which 
are responsible for different tasks such as data processing, user interaction or storage 
management. NeuroClip has been architected into two main modules to stre amline the 
computational heavy-lifting part of video processing out of the interactive logic part of the 
search and retrieval part. This holds true in the modular design, which ensures that it is 
scalable, maintainable and allows to update certain elements  such as the model of 
embedding without disrupting the entire system. 
Each of the modules is described as follows: 
 User Registration & Login 
   This module handles the security and identity verification of the system with the help 
of Supabase Auth. Instead of solely using the social login from the third-party, the system 
provides a robust flow of email-and-password authentication. It has a compulsory email 
16  
verification process in order to make sure that the user is genuine and there are no spam 
accounts. Additionally, a secure "Forgot Password" mechanism is added that can be used 
by users for account recovery through email emails. This module ensures that any video 
data, processing histories, are strictly isolated and cannot be accessed by any 
authenticated owner. 
 Video Upload & Management 
   The main way of ingesting data is the Video Upload & Management module. It gives 
functionality to the user to upload video file from their local devices to the cloud storage. 
Before processing, we have added a trimming feature in this module in which a user could 
select and crop specific portions of a video they would like to analyze instead of 
processing entire files. Additionally, it contains a dashboard feature that fetches and 
presents a list of previously uploaded and processed videos so that the users can manage 
them, replay them, or delete the history efficiently. 
 Video Summarization 
   This is the core processing module of the system, which is driven by Vector 
Embeddings. As opposed to traditional frequency -based summarization, this module is 
based on deep learning models (such as Sentence -Transformers) that can turn video 
transcripts and visual context into high -dimensional vectors. By analyzing these 
embeddings, the system determines semantically important cluster s and nuanced 
moments of the video that represent the essential topics of the video. It has these segments 
to be scored on the basis of their semantic density and their relevance to the intent of the 
user. High scoring parts are then mathematically selected and stitched together to produce 
a brief coherent summary of the original with the context preserved. 
 Video Summarization in Text 
   To complement the visual summary, a textual explanation of the video content is 
created in this module. Making use of the  identical concept of the embedding, it then 
groups together related sentence vectors to abstract the video's narrative. It then uses 
Natural Language Processing (NLP) to produce a structured summary of the text in the 
form of timestamped bullet points to the user. This means that people can easily scan 
through the key points of the video and jump to particular timestamps, without having to 
watch the entire video. 
 Sensitive Information Blurring 
   To overcome privacy concerns that are ingrained in public video data, this module plays 
17  
out as an automated redaction system. Leveraging the YOLOv8 object detection model, 
it looks at frames of video on a frame -by-frame basis to detect sensitive objects such as 
human faces, national ID cards and vehicle license pla tes. Once these objects have been 
detected the system applies a dynamic blurring filter specifically to these regions of 
interest (ROI). This guarantees that the identities of people and private information isn't 
disclosed in the end result and makes the video safe to be distributed more broadly. 
 Video Compression 
   This module optimizes the storage and transmission of the processed videos. After all 
summarization and blurring are done, this system uses ffmpeg to re-encode the video file. 
It uses efficient compression algorithms (like H.264/H.265) to compress  the file size by 
some 75 % while using minimum visual noticeable quality. This reduction drastically 
reduces the amount of bandwidth consumed and storage costs as it allows for the final 
summaries to be lightweight and easy to share across a variety of different platforms with 
no noticeable loss in video quality. 
 Functional Requirements 
   Functional requirements specify the specific behaviours and functions of the system, 
specifying what the system shou ld do to satisfy user requirements. For NeuroClip these 
requirements are centered around the holistic ingestion, processing and retrieval of video 
content. The system is designed to allow its users to have a robust platform for uploading 
videos, creating A I-based summaries, providing privacy through automatic blurring, and 
retrieving content through semantic search. The basic functional requirements include 
authentication, video management, intelligent summarization, and safe playback. 
Each of the key functional requirements is described in detail below: 
 User Registration and Authentication 
   The system shall provide mean of new user account registration using valid email 
address, and password. It needs to implement a verification process in which a 
confirmation link is sent to the user's e -mail to verify their identity before their account 
is activated. Additionally, the system needs to have a secure login option for returning 
users and a "Forgot Password" option for account recovery. 
 Video Upload and Preprocessing 
   The system shall allow authenticated users to upload video files (in file formats such as 
.mp4, .mkv) to the cloud storage. During the uploading process, the system should enable 
18  
the several web users to choose to trim the video in order to sel ect the specific segment 
that they want to be processed. Once uploaded, the system shall automatically launch a 
background job to verify the file format and size and queue it to the transcription and 
generation of the embeddings. 
 Automated Video Summarization 
   The system shall automatically create a brief presentation of the uploaded video. This 
feature has to leverage deep learning models to detect important moments which are 
based on semantic importance and queries defined by users. The system shall ext ract 
relevant video segments, and stitch them together to create a coherent video summary 
clip containing the essential information from the original video. 
 Semantic Search and Retrieval 
   The system shall provide search interface with natural language queries from the user. 
It has to use the vector embeddings to be able to semantically match the query that the 
user is typing with the transcripts of the videos. The system shall provide a list of available 
relevant video clips based on their semantic simila rity score, so that users can find 
specific information without exact matches of relevant keywords. 
 Sensitive Information Redaction 
   The system shall automatically detect sensitive visual information of video frames, 
namely human faces, license plate, an d ID card. Upon its detection, the system has to 
apply a blurring filter on these areas in order to uphold the privacy of people. This process 
needs to be automated and applied to the final output video before they are available for 
viewing or sharing. 
 Video Compression and Playback 
   The system shall compress processed videos in order to reduce the file size at acceptable 
visual quality for streaming. It must support an inbuilt video player that gives users the 
ability to stream the automatically generate d summaries and retrieved clips directly 
within the web application. The player must support the standard playback controls (i.e. 
play, pause, volume, fullscreen) and the player must be able to load the content 
efficiently, to make sure of a good user experience.

experience, scalability and long term viability of NeuroClip. The system focuses on 
Usability, Reliability, Performance, Security and Scalability to meet the processing of high

 Usability

two takes without having to go out side of the system to obtain documentation. The 
interface for cutting videos and reading summaries should be self -explanatory by using 
common icons and labels. 
 Reliability & Availability 
   Reliability guarantees that the system works properly and consiste ntly over the years 
without breaking down when errors occur. 
Rationale: Video processing is resource intensive; failures during long uploads/  
processing job is frustrating. 
Measurement: Uptime during operational hours of the system should be 99.5% In the 
event of processing failure (e.g. a corrupted video file), the system does not crash but 
instead withdraws a readable and comprehensible error message and allows the user to 
retry the operation with no data loss. 
 Performance & Reactiveness 
   Performance is the responsiveness and the speed of the system under expected load 
conditions.

 Portability & Compatibility

   System modeling and design is a very crucial part of the software development process 
where the requirements specifications are translated into the construction blueprint of 
software. It involves the crafting of abstract representations of the system to understand its 
structure and behavior and its interactions before it is actually implemented. This phase is 
essential for identifying potential design flaws, ensures that the sy stem scales and makes 
sure that architecture of the system is clear for all stakeholders. By using the various 
standard modelling diagrams, developers can visualize the data flow and user interactions, 
as well as the internal logic, and this can help to cr eate a more robust and efficient 
development process. 
The system being discussed, NeuroClip, is an intelligent video processing system that aims 
at improving content retrieval using AI -driven summarization and semantic search. It 
integrates complex compone nts like automated transcription, deep learning -based 
embedding generation and object detection for privacy protection. In order to properly 
model this system, a number of Unified Modeling Language (UML) and structured 
diagrams have been applied. These include Use Case Diagrams to show user interactions, 
Activity Diagrams to show logic flow, Data Flow Diagram (DFD) to show information 
flow, and Sequence Diagrams to show the working of objects at a specific time. 
Additionally, architectural diagrams also give you a high-level view of frontend, backend 
and database integration. 
This chapter is structured so that it helps obtain a big picture of the design of the system. It 
starts with the System Design and Analysis followed by detailed behavioral models such 
as Use Case Diagrams and Activity Diagrams. Data-oriented models such as DFDs are then

Diagrams. Finally, the structure is presented with detailed information using Class
